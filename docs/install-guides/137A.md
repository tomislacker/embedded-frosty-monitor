# Install guide — Frosty Factory 137A

This is the reference install procedure for frosty-monitor. The 117A and
127A guides ([117A-delta.md](117A-delta.md), [127A-delta.md](127A-delta.md))
are written as deltas against this document — read this one first regardless
of which machine you're installing on.

> **Before you start:** read [../safety.md](../safety.md) in full. Every
> electrical step below assumes the machine is **unplugged and locked out**.
> Line-voltage work in the junction box is for a qualified technician only.

## What you're installing

The logger core (IP65 enclosure, mounted outside the cabinet or on the rear
panel exterior) plus its sensor pods: two CT clamps, two vibration pods, five
temperature probes (four DS18B20 + one thermocouple), a drip-tube drop
counter, and two-to-four AC-presence opto taps. See
[docs/architecture/overview.md](../architecture/overview.md) for what each
piece is and [../hardware/wiring-and-pinmap.md](../hardware/wiring-and-pinmap.md)
for pin-level wiring.

## Reference: 137A machine layout

- Electrical: 115V single-phase, 20A dedicated circuit.
- Cabinet: removable left, right, and rear panels.
- Compressor, air-cooled condenser, and fan sit at the rear base. Air
  **intake is at the rear**, discharge out the sides — 8" side/rear
  clearance is required for airflow, and must be restored after install.
- Junction box (low at rear, near the compressor) holds: 24V contactor
  (F0478), ~2-minute anti-short-cycle timer (F4998), 40VA transformer
  (F4995), run/start capacitors, and the standby thermostat (F0401).
- Belt-driven Franklin drive motor, poly-V belt (380J6), and flywheel at the
  upper rear drive the beater bar.
- TCC (torque control cutoff): the motor is pivot-mounted and rocks against
  a tension spring as load increases; that motion actuates microswitch
  F0346. The adjustment screw is reachable through an opening in the
  left/rear panel.
- High-pressure switch: F0661.
- Hopper float switch: F0811.
- The drip tube below the faceplate is a designed telltale for rear seal
  failure — not a defect, but worth noting in the deployment record if it's
  actively dripping at install time.
- Electrical chain: the beater motor is fed **directly** from the front
  3-position toggle switch — no contactor in that leg. The compressor and
  condenser fan are on the contactor leg. The 24V control chain is
  transformer → TCC microswitch → anti-short-cycle timer → HP switch →
  contactor coil.

## Procedure

### 1. Pre-install: bench-provision the DAQ

Do this before going to the machine, not in front of it.

1. Format the SD card fresh.
2. Power the logger core and connect over USB serial.
3. Set the RTC: `SETTIME <UTC ISO-8601>`, e.g.
   `SETTIME 2026-09-14T10:00:00Z`. The RTC is the time source of truth for
   the whole deployment — see
   [../firmware/architecture.md](../firmware/architecture.md#configloader-boot--cli).
4. Set deployment identity: `ID` with deployment ID, machine model (`137A`),
   machine serial, and technician name.
5. Write/confirm `config.json` on the card with deployment metadata and any
   sample-rate overrides. See
   [../firmware/data-format-spec.md](../firmware/data-format-spec.md) for
   the exact shape.
6. Confirm `STATUS` reports the expected deployment ID, model, and a healthy
   card.
7. Verify all three status LEDs light on command (recording heartbeat,
   error, card-capacity) before leaving the bench.

### 2. Panel removal

> **WARNING:** Confirm the machine is unplugged and locked out per
> [../safety.md](../safety.md) before removing any panel that exposes the
> junction box or drive components.

Remove the rear panel and the left side panel. These expose the junction
box, contactor, TCC microswitch adjustment area, and the compressor/vibration
mounting areas used below.

### 3. CT install in the junction box

> **WARNING:** Machine unplugged and locked out. CT clamps are non-invasive
> — no conductor is cut or spliced — but you are working inside an
> energized-when-plugged-in junction box.

| CT | Location | Conductor |
|---|---|---|
| CT-1 | Junction box, beater-motor leg | The single conductor from the front 3-position toggle feeding the beater motor |
| CT-2 | Junction box, compressor feed | The single conductor at the contactor (F0478) output feeding the compressor |

Clamp around **one conductor only** at each point — never the whole cord
(clamping the whole cord/cable, hot+neutral together, reads near-zero net
current). Dress CT leads away from moving parts and secure with zip ties.

### 4. AC-presence taps

| Tap | Node | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Opto 1 | Beater switch leg (120V), at the front toggle output | Required |
| Opto 2 | 24V contactor coil terminals (F0478) | Required |
| Opto 3 (optional) | TCC-microswitch (F0346) output | Adds finer control-chain state resolution |
| Opto 4 (optional) | HP-switch (F0661) output | Adds finer control-chain state resolution |

Opto modules are isolated AC-presence sensors, not multimeters — they report
whether the node is energized, not a voltage value. See
[../firmware/architecture.md](../firmware/architecture.md#ac-presence-edge-counting-not-digitalread)
for why the firmware treats this as a pulse train, not a steady logic level
— nothing to do on-site here, just be aware a scope on the GPIO will show
pulses, not a flat line, and that's correct.

### 5. Vibration pods

> **WARNING:** Keep hands and cabling clear of the belt and flywheel;
> confirm the machine is unplugged before reaching near either.

**Pod A (beater drive):** Clean and degrease a flat spot on the drive-motor
mount or bearing-plate area near the flywheel, upper rear of the cabinet.
Mount with an epoxy stud or VHB adhesive pad. Dress the cable **away from
the belt and flywheel** — route it along the frame, not across the belt
path, and secure with zip ties well clear of any moving part.

**Pod B (compressor):** Mount on the compressor shell dome with adhesive.
Keep it away from the discharge line — that line runs hot (see step 6) and
adhesive-mounted electronics near it are a heat-cycling risk.

### 6. Temperature probes

> **WARNING:** The compressor discharge line runs above 90°C. Let the
> machine cool if it was recently running, and dress the thermocouple leads
> with high-temperature sleeving — standard hookup wire insulation can fail
> against this line.

| Probe | Location | Mount |
|---|---|---|
| DS18B20 #1 | Freezing-cylinder jacket, adjacent to the OEM thermostat (F0401) capillary bulb | Strap/tape in contact with the jacket |
| DS18B20 #2 | Condenser air intake, rear | Zip-tied in the intake airstream |
| DS18B20 #3 | Side discharge airstream | Zip-tied in the discharge airstream |
| DS18B20 #4 | Ambient, inside rear compartment | Positioned away from the compressor and discharge line so it reads compartment ambient, not local heat |
| K-type thermocouple | Compressor discharge line, a few inches from the compressor | Washer or hose-clamp mount; high-temp lead sleeving required |

### 7. Leak-sensing add-ons

**Drop counter (base config, required):** Clip the IR slot-type optical drop
counter onto the drip tube outlet below the faceplate — the machine's
factory-designed telltale for rear cylinder seal wear (see the machine
layout notes above). Mount it so the drip path stays fully open; **the
sensor must never restrict or plug the tube's drainage.** Route the
pulse-output pigtail to a JST detach point alongside the other pods, back to
GPIO 21 (`DRIP_PULSE`) at the logger.

**Moisture pad (optional):** If installed, place the capacitive moisture pad
under the drip tray / machine footprint to catch pooling separate from the
drip-tube path. Route its pigtail clear of foot traffic.

**Gas sensor (optional, experimental):** If installed, mount the gas-sensor
pod low in the compressor compartment — refrigerant vapors are heavier than
air — away from the direct condenser airflow blast. This sensor is
experimental; do not present its readings to a customer as diagnostic
without bench validation (see bom.md risks). The primary refrigerant-loss
signature remains compressor short-cycling plus reduced condenser ΔT, both
already covered by the base channels.

### 8. Cable routing

Route all pod cables (including any leak-sensing add-ons) to the DAQ
enclosure, which mounts outside the cabinet or on the rear panel exterior —
not inside the sealed compartment. Tighten cable glands at the enclosure.
JST detach points stay **inside** the cabinet at each pod, so pods can be
disconnected for service or panel removal without opening the enclosure.

### 9. Record the channel map

Before closing panels, record into `config.json` (or directly via the CLI if
supported at this stage):

- Which DS18B20 ROM address is at which location (cylinder, condenser-in,
  condenser-out, ambient).
- CT-1 and CT-2 physical positions (confirm beater vs. compressor legs
  weren't swapped).
- Any optional opto taps (3/4) that were wired.

This becomes the `channel_map` in `manifest.json` — see
[../firmware/data-format-spec.md](../firmware/data-format-spec.md). Getting
this right on-site is much cheaper than reverse-engineering it from data
later.

### 10. Close up and verify

1. Reinstall panels; confirm the 8" side/rear airflow clearance is restored
   — condenser airflow is not optional, a blocked condenser will show up as
   a false failure signature in the data.
2. Plug the machine back in.
3. Plug the DAQ into a **separate** outlet from the machine's circuit — see
   [../safety.md](../safety.md) for why.
4. Confirm the recording LED shows a heartbeat blink.
5. Connect over serial and run `STATUS` to confirm active recording, correct
   deployment ID, and healthy card capacity.

### 11. Journaling sticker and staff briefing

Apply the QR journaling sticker to the front panel near the event button.
Brief on-site staff: **press the button any time something seems off** —
unusual noise, texture, a leak, anything — even without further detail.
Optionally, scan the QR code afterward to add a description, photos, or
video, but the button press alone is a complete, timestamped incident
record on its own; don't let "I don't have time to fill out the form" stop
someone from pressing it.

### 12. Pickup

1. Run `STATUS` to confirm the deployment recorded for its full intended
   window.
2. Press the event button once as a deliberate end-of-visit marker so the
   data has an unambiguous "tech was on-site, pickup starting" timestamp.
3. Power down the unit.
4. Pull the SD card.
5. Remove pods and DAQ enclosure; note anything in the uninstall record
   (contamination on a pod, a probe that came loose, panel condition) that
   should feed back into the next install.
6. Reinstall any panels removed for uninstall.

## Expected signals on a healthy machine

Use this table to sanity-check the first hour of data at install, and as a
baseline when comparing a suspect machine's data later.

| Channel | Healthy-machine expectation |
|---|---|
| `current_beater_a` | Steady in the low-single-digit amps, with a slow rise over the freeze cycle as product thickens against the beater |
| `current_compressor_a` | Duty-cycling on and off, each on/off cycle **≥30 seconds** — cycles much shorter than that suggest short-cycling |
| `temp_cond_in_c` vs `temp_cond_out_c` | A clear ΔT present whenever the compressor is running (condenser doing work); little to no ΔT while the compressor is off |
| `temp_discharge_c` | Hot (well above ambient, consistent with the >90°C warning above) whenever the compressor is running |
| `tcc_satisfied` | Toggles over the freeze cycle as product reaches target consistency — not stuck permanently on or off |
| `beater_on` / `compressor_cmd` | Track the physical switch/contactor state exactly; a mismatch against the corresponding current channel is itself a signal worth flagging |
| `drip_rate_cpm` | Zero or near-zero; occasional single drops during a cleaning cycle are normal. A sustained nonzero rate outside cleaning is the rear-seal-wear signature the drop counter exists to catch |
