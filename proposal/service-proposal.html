1Executive summary

Your frozen-drink machine is a revenue anchor, not just a piece of equipment — and when it fails, it fails expensively, on a schedule it chooses. Diagnosing it today is largely guesswork: a technician listens, looks, and maybe catches the problem, if it happens to act up during the hour they're standing there.

FrostSight instruments your machine with 12+ sensor channels — motor and compressor electrical load, vibration at the drive and compressor, six temperature points through the refrigeration circuit, and control-system state — and records continuously, 24/7, for up to two weeks. At the end of that window you get a written diagnosis: what's actually wrong, and what it will take to fix it. Your staff can flag anything unusual the moment it happens with a single button press, so the report explains real events, not just averages.

The result: you fix the right thing the first time, you make repair-or-replace calls on data instead of a hunch, and you catch developing failures before they take the machine down on a Friday night.

2The problem: what downtime and guesswork actually cost

A frozen-drink machine is a small, hard-working refrigeration system with a mechanical beater bar layered on top, running long hours in a hot kitchen or back bar. When it goes down, the cost isn't just the repair bill — it's lost sales, spoiled product, and a service call priced for an emergency instead of a scheduled visit.

$2,500–$8,000Typical all-in cost of an emergency refrigeration failure — labor, expedited parts, and lost product, industry figures.
$500–$1,500/nightRevenue at risk when a machine goes down on a busy night, at typical utilization and rated drink capacity.
$600–$1,700 vs. $4,600–$5,300A compressor replacement against a new machine — the classic "50% rule" repair-or-replace decision our data is built to de-risk.
$250–$500The retail value of a single dumped batch, before counting the liquor already in it.

Standard service calls typically run $100–$1,200 with a 2-hour minimum; after-hours dispatch adds $150–$350, and emergency labor runs 1.5–2× standard rates — on top of whatever the actual repair costs.

The intermittent-failure problem. The failures that cost the most are the ones that vanish the moment a technician is standing there. A knock that only happens on the third freeze cycle of the night, a compressor that short-cycles only after two hours of hot-kitchen ambient, a temperature drift that only shows up under Friday-night volume — a one-hour service call cannot catch these. A two-week continuous recording does, because it's running when the problem happens, not just when someone is watching.

For comparison, renting a bare industrial data logger — no installation, no analysis, no report — typically runs $125–$350/week on its own. A comparable preventive-maintenance contract runs $300–$1,200/year per piece of equipment and still amounts to two blind visits a year, not continuous data.

4How an engagement works

  1. Initial call. We confirm machine model(s), count, and scheduling. No site visit required to scope this.
  2. Install visit (~1 hour). A qualified technician installs the monitoring hardware. The machine is briefly offline during install; nothing is mounted in the food-product zone, and the monitor plugs into its own separate outlet — it does not interfere with the machine's operation once running.
  3. 1–2 weeks of unattended monitoring. The machine runs normally. Staff press the journal button any time something seems off, and can optionally scan a QR code to add a note, photo, or video — all of it lands in one shared record, not scattered across whoever's phone happened to be nearby.
  4. Pickup. The technician returns, retrieves the monitoring hardware, and closes out the deployment.
  5. Written diagnostic report, within 5 business days. See the sample report linked below.
  6. Findings call (30 minutes). We walk through the report together and answer questions.

7Safety, data handling & liability summary

All installs are performed by a qualified technician, with the machine unplugged and locked out for the duration of any electrical work. Nothing is mounted in the food-product zone. The monitor itself is electrically isolated and separately powered from its own outlet — it never taps or shares the machine's dedicated circuit, and no line voltage is ever present inside the monitoring enclosure.

We collect machine telemetry only — electrical load, vibration, temperature, and control-state data, plus timestamped button presses. No audio, no video from us, and no personal or point-of-sale data. You receive all of your data; we retain a copy to build and improve our diagnostic reference across machines. Full detail is in the attached data handling summary.

This is a diagnostic service. Findings and recommendations are exactly that — recommendations. Any repair work is quoted and performed separately.

9FAQ

Does it affect my machine's operation? No. It's sensors only — nothing intercepts or controls the machine, and nothing sits in the food-product zone.

What if the problem doesn't happen during the monitoring window? You can extend monitoring at $175/week, and even a "quiet" two weeks has value — it establishes a healthy baseline to compare against later.

Do my staff need training? One button. Press it if something seems off. That's the whole ask; the QR form for photos and notes is optional.

What machines are supported? The Frosty Factory line (117A, 127A, 137A) and similar frozen-drink machines. Other makes are assessed on request.

Will this catch every possible problem? No monitoring window guarantees a fault occurs during it. What it does guarantee is a real 24/7 record instead of a one-hour snapshot, which is where most intermittent failures actually get caught.

Who installs and removes the equipment? A qualified technician, on both ends of the engagement.

Does the monitor need its own outlet? Yes — it's a firm requirement, covered in the safety section above.

What happens to my data? You get all of it. See the data handling summary for the full answer.
